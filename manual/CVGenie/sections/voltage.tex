\manualchapter{Voltage, byte values, and toggles}{sec:voltage}
\subsection{Continuous entries}
For an ordinary entry, CV Genie maps \textbf{0--10 V} linearly to the two
endpoint values in the game map and repeatedly writes the result while the
input is connected. Values become integers; there is no smoothing or
musical pitch quantization. The ranges are byte values, not units such as
hertz, percent, or screen pixels guaranteed by Genie.

For endpoints $L$ and $H$, a voltage $v$ in the intended range produces
approximately
\[
\text{byte}=\left\lfloor L+(H-L)\frac{v}{10}\right\rfloor.
\]
Floating-point arithmetic can affect a boundary value. A decreasing endpoint
pair reverses the direction of the mapping.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Mapped endpoints} & \textbf{0 V} & \textbf{5 V} & \textbf{10 V} \\
\midrule
0--255 & 0 & 127 & 255 \\
0--2 & 0 & 1 & 2 \\
8--255 (Max Bomb Count) & 8 & 131 & 255 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For 0--2, the highest value is reached only at the upper endpoint. Use a
steady voltage source when you need a particular value instead of relying
on an LFO to linger there.

Continuous input voltage is limited to \textbf{0--10 V} before conversion.
Negative voltage selects the first endpoint; voltage above 10 V selects the
second. Offset and attenuate a bipolar LFO to use its full sweep across the
range. Non-finite signals are ignored.

\subsection{Toggle entries}
Entries marked \textbf{T} alternate between the table's \textbf{First} and
\textbf{Second} values on each rising trigger. The gate activates around
\textbf{2 V} and must fall to about \textbf{0.1 V} or below before another
trigger. The falling edge does not write a value, and a held high gate does
not continuously enforce it.

A newly assigned row starts with First selected internally; its first detected
rising edge writes Second, and the next writes First. Most entries use 0 and
1. Spawn Powerup instead alternates 0/46, Reverb (Pulse) alternates 128/1,
and Potion Type alternates 0/2.

The toggle tracks your trigger sequence, not the game's current byte. Game
logic can change the byte after a write. Saving the Rack patch preserves the
row's toggle choice so the next rising edge continues the sequence.
