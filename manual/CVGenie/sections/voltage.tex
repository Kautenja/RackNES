\manualchapter{Voltage, byte values, and toggles}{sec:voltage}
\subsection{Continuous entries}
For an ordinary entry, CV Genie maps \textbf{0--10 V} linearly to the two
endpoint values in the game map and repeatedly writes the result while the
input is connected. Values become integers; there is no smoothing or
musical pitch quantization. The ranges are byte values, not units such as
hertz, percent, or screen pixels guaranteed by Genie.

For endpoints $L$ and $H$, a voltage $v$ in the intended range produces
approximately
\[
\text{byte}=\left\lfloor L+(H-L)\frac{v}{10}\right\rfloor.
\]
Floating-point arithmetic can affect a boundary value. A decreasing endpoint
pair reverses the direction of the mapping.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Mapped endpoints} & \textbf{0 V} & \textbf{5 V} & \textbf{10 V} \\
\midrule
0--255 & 0 & 127 & 255 \\
0--2 & 0 & 1 & 2 \\
8--255 (Max Bomb Count) & 8 & 131 & 255 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For 0--2, the highest value is reached only at the upper endpoint. Use a
steady voltage source when you need a particular value instead of relying
on an LFO to linger there.

\textbf{Keep continuous input voltages between 0 and 10 V.} The input path
rescales without clamping. Negative or excessive voltages can convert to
unintended byte values; they are not safely limited to the table's endpoints.
Offset and attenuate a bipolar LFO before connecting it.

\subsection{Toggle entries}
Entries marked \textbf{T} in the tables alternate a per-row state on each
rising trigger: 0 to 1, then 1 to 0. The gate activates around \textbf{2 V}
and must fall to about \textbf{0.1 V} or below before another trigger.
The falling edge does not write zero, and a held high gate does not
continuously enforce the value.

A newly created row starts with toggle state 0, so its first detected rise
writes 1. This is an internal state; it is not read from the game. The game
may immediately change the byte again. A missed-looking change can be game
behavior, and more than one trigger may be needed to see an effect.

\textbf{Toggle entries always write 0 or 1}, even when their table endpoints
say something else. In particular, Spawn Powerup, Reverb (Pulse), and
Potion Type do not span their full listed endpoints in toggle mode.
